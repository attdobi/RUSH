Reinforcement learning Using SME feedback and High-reasoning models

Cheap models label. Hard cases escalate. SMEs decide — and the policy learns.

A panel of low-cost models labels everything against an SME-owned policy graph; disagreements climb to a high-reasoning judge, the residue goes to experts, and every accepted ruling becomes the next policy version. Decision quality grows run over run.

        Methodology: train drives updates, test drives reported metrics
        

          

            
Train / test discipline

            
Learn from train, report on test. Training disagreements drive policy updates; held-out test images produce the reported score.

          

          
        

        

          

            TRAIN → drives PROMPT UPDATES
            Hardest training disagreements become policy work.
            
Prompt/policy updates are driven by boundary cases, lack-of-consensus rows, and SME/model misalignments in the training split.

          

          

            TEST → drives REPORTED METRICS
            Held-out decision quality is the reported score.
            
Reported metrics should be computed on the test split after the round, separate from the training rows that suggested updates.

About

        
System, theory, and next steps.

      

      

        System
      

        
      

      

      

        Theory
      

        
Theory

        
RUSH turns review judgment into a measurable policy loop.

      

      

        
RUSH stands for Reinforcement learning Using SME feedback and High-reasoning models. It captures the operating loop: high-reasoning models propose, subject-matter experts review and approve or reject, the policy graph updates, and future runs are graded against versioned truth.

        
Methodology

        
The system starts with a cold-start policy graph, not a free-form prompt blob. Bulk LLM labeling cites policy nodes, compares against SME truth, and turns repeated ambiguity into proposed graph diffs.

        
Cost and quality frame

        

          

            
DimensionWorking assumptionImplication

            
              
Synthesis costHigh-reasoning labels cost ~10-40x cheap-tier labels per imageUse expensive synthesis only where it buys better policy patches or audit explanations.

              
Label qualityReasoning-tier models can improve boundary handling and policy-node citation quality.Measure by scored runs, not vibes: accuracy, F1, false positives, false negatives, abstains, and review burden.

              
Review allocationConsensus rows are cheaper to accept; split or majority-wrong rows deserve SME attention.Route scarce human time to the highest expected policy-learning value.

            
          

        

        
SME-feedback loop

        
SMEs do not rubber-stamp model labels. They adjudicate evidence, clarify boundaries, and approve durable graph changes that future runs can cite.

        
Related framing: active learning, human-in-the-loop annotation, weak supervision, model auditing, and dataset governance. RUSH adapts those ideas to policy-versioned visual classification with explicit cost and review-burden accounting.
